## Grinding is often invisible to the person doing it

Many people grind or clench their teeth at night, and only find out when a partner hears it or a dentist spots wear on the teeth. This habit is called **bruxism**. Occasional grinding is common, but frequent grinding can damage teeth and strain the jaw.

## Signs you may be grinding

![Signs of teeth grinding: jaw ache, worn teeth, headaches, sensitivity](/Resources/blog/teeth-grinding-bruxism-night-guard-signs.webp)

- **A tired or sore jaw** in the morning
- **Headaches**, often around the temples
- **Flat, worn or chipped teeth**
- **Tooth sensitivity** with no obvious cavity
- **Clicking or tight jaw joints**
- **Tongue or cheek ridges** where you press them against your teeth
- **A partner who hears grinding** at night

These signs can have other causes, so an examination is the only way to confirm.

## Why does it happen?

The exact cause is not always clear, and it often has several contributors:

- **Stress and anxiety**
- **Poor sleep**, including snoring and sleep apnoea
- **Caffeine, alcohol and tobacco**
- **Bite problems** or missing teeth, in some cases
- **Certain medicines**

## What grinding can do to your teeth

Over time, grinding can wear down enamel, cause cracks, loosen fillings and crowns, and contribute to jaw pain. It can also put extra load on implants and new restorations, so mention it if you are planning an implant or crown.

## How a night guard helps

A night guard (occlusal splint) is a custom-made, comfortable plastic guard worn at night. It does not stop you from grinding, but it **cushions and spreads the forces**, so your teeth wear against the guard rather than against each other.

- **Custom-made** from an impression or scan, so it fits comfortably. Shop-bought guards can be bulky and may not protect well.
- **Easy to care for.** Rinse and brush it daily and keep it in a case.
- **Reviewed regularly.** We check it for wear and fit at your visits.

## Other things that can help

- **Manage stress**: exercise, a wind-down routine and enough sleep help many people.
- **Cut back on caffeine and alcohol**, especially in the evening.
- **Avoid chewing gum and pen caps**, which keep jaw muscles working.
- **Relax your jaw during the day.** Aim for lips together, teeth apart.
- **Check for sleep problems** if you snore loudly or wake unrefreshed. Speak to your doctor.

## When to see a dentist

If you notice worn teeth, jaw pain, repeated chipped fillings or morning headaches, book a check. We will look at your teeth, your bite and your jaw joints, and talk through the options.
