Dental Implants

Missing a Tooth? Your Options: Implant, Bridge or Denture

  3. How to choose

Does a gap matter if it does not hurt?

Often it does, even if you cannot see it. After a tooth is lost, neighbouring teeth can tilt toward the gap, the opposing tooth can drift, and the bone where the root used to be slowly shrinks. Chewing, bite and appearance can change over time. Replacing a missing tooth is not always urgent, but it is worth planning.

The main options

Dental implant

A titanium post placed in the jawbone with a crown on top.

  • Often a good fit when: you have enough bone and healthy gums, and want a solution that does not involve neighbouring teeth.
  • Pros: works like a natural tooth, helps preserve the bone where the root was, and neighbouring teeth stay untouched.
  • Things to know: it involves a small surgery, needs enough bone and good health, and takes several months from start to finish in most cases.

Dental bridge

A false tooth held in place by crowns on the teeth on either side of the gap.

  • Often a good fit when: the neighbouring teeth already need crowns, or an implant is not possible.
  • Pros: usually quicker than an implant, with no surgery.
  • Things to know: the neighbouring teeth are reduced to hold the bridge, the bone under the gap can still shrink, and cleaning under the bridge needs extra care.

Removable denture

A removable set of replacement teeth, partial or complete.

  • Often a good fit when: several teeth are missing, or other options are not suitable for now.
  • Pros: non-surgical and often a lower first cost.
  • Things to know: it can feel loose over time, needs regular adjustment and daily cleaning, and does not preserve bone like an implant does.

How to choose

There is no single right answer. Consider:

  1. Which tooth is missing? Front or back, one tooth or several.
  2. Condition of the neighbouring teeth. Are they healthy or already heavily filled?
  3. Bone and gum health. An exam and 3D scan show what is possible.
  4. Your health and habits. For example, tobacco use and diabetes affect planning.
  5. Time and comfort. How long are you prepared to wait, and are you comfortable with surgery?
  6. The full picture of cost over time, including maintenance and follow-up. Ask for a written estimate.

Read more about candidacy in Am I a candidate for a dental implant? and visit our dental implants page and crowns and bridges page.

What if I leave the gap?

For a back tooth that is rarely seen, some people choose to leave the gap. That is a valid choice if the bite is stable and the neighbouring teeth are not drifting. We would suggest a periodic check so changes are caught early.

Questions to ask any dentist

  • Which options suit my teeth, and why?
  • What are the risks and limits of each?
  • How long will it take, and how many visits?
  • What maintenance will it need?
  • What is the full estimate, including follow-up?
