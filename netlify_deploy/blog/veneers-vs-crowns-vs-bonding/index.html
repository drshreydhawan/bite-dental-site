Veneers vs. Crowns vs. Bonding: Which Fits Your Teeth?

Three treatments, three different jobs

People often use "veneer", "crown" and "bonding" as if they mean the same thing. They do not. Each one covers a tooth in a different way and suits a different problem. The right choice depends on how much healthy tooth you have, what you want to change, and how long you want it to last.

Dental bonding

What it is: A tooth-coloured resin is shaped directly onto the tooth and set with a light. It is usually done in a single visit.

Often suits:

  • Small chips, gaps or uneven edges
  • Small areas of discolouration
  • Minor reshaping

Things to know:

  • Needs little or no removal of tooth structure
  • Resin can stain and chip over time and may need repair or polishing
  • Less suited to large changes

Porcelain or ceramic veneers

What it is: A thin shell of ceramic bonded to the front of a tooth. Veneers are made in a dental lab from a digital or physical impression, so there are usually two or more visits.

Often suits:

  • Teeth that are stained and do not respond well to whitening
  • Mild shape or size changes, minor gaps, or slight misalignment
  • A planned "smile makeover" of the front teeth

Things to know:

  • A thin layer of enamel is usually removed so the veneer sits naturally. That step is not reversible.
  • Ceramic resists staining well, but veneers can chip or debond, and may need replacement over time.
  • Habits such as biting nails, ice or hard objects can damage them. A night guard may be advised if you grind.
  • Teeth with large fillings or weak structure may be better served by a crown.

Crowns (caps)

What it is: A crown covers the whole tooth, above the gumline. Zirconia and porcelain-fused-to-metal are common options.

Often suits:

  • Teeth that are heavily decayed, broken or weakened
  • Teeth after root canal treatment, which often need protection
  • Teeth with large old fillings
  • Replacing a missing tooth as part of an implant or bridge

Things to know:

  • More tooth is shaped than for a veneer, but you get complete coverage and strength.
  • A well-made crown can last many years with good care.
  • Our guide to crowns goes into materials and care.

How do you choose?

Bonding Veneer Crown
Covers Part of the tooth Front surface Whole tooth
Tooth reduction Little or none A thin layer More
Typical visits Often one Usually two or more Usually two or more
Often chosen for Small fixes Cosmetic upgrades Strength and protection
Staining over time More likely Less likely Less likely

Which option is right depends on your mouth, so this table is only a starting point.

What if I only want whiter teeth?

If your teeth are healthy and simply stained, professional whitening is usually the simplest, least invasive step. We often suggest trying it before anything permanent. See our teeth whitening page and our whitening guide.

Questions to ask before you decide

  1. Is my tooth healthy enough for this option?
  2. What would you remove, and is that reversible?
  3. How long might it last, and what might it need later?
  4. What are the other options, including doing nothing?
  5. What is the full estimate, including follow-up care?

A good dentist will welcome these questions. At our veneers page you can see how we plan a smile design, and we are happy to explain the pros and cons for your teeth before you commit to anything.
